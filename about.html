About

I came for the spreadsheets and stayed for the decisions.

Every number in a model is an argument about the future. I like the work of making that argument honest: finding the assumption that matters, testing it, and writing it down so a room of busy people can decide.

01The journey

  1. 01
    Before 2020

    Learning the language

    Finance and economics at university, and a first internship where I discovered that the hardest part of a valuation is the paragraph explaining it.

  2. 02
    2021 — 2024

    The banking years

    M&A taught me speed, precision, and how a process really runs: from the first teaser to the signing call after midnight. It also taught me that trust is built in the details nobody asked for.

  3. 03
    2024 — Present

    Thinking like an owner

    Private equity changed the question from “will this deal close?” to “should we own this for five years?” Underwriting, value-creation plans, and living with the consequences of a model.

  4. 04
    Now

    Building with AI

    I build AI workflows that compress the grunt work of diligence, like reading, summarizing, and cross-checking, so more of a deal team’s time goes to judgment.

02Things I believe

  1. A good memo fits on one page. Everything else is appendix.
  2. Every model is a story with numbers attached. Know which story you are telling.
  3. Underwrite the downside first. Upside is a gift; downside is a bill.
  4. Management quality shows up in small things, like how fast the data room answers and how honestly bad news travels.
  5. Speed is a feature. Judgment is the product.
  6. AI should remove drudgery, not responsibility. A person still signs the work.
  7. Reputation compounds faster than capital.

03Where I’m headed

Over the next decade I want to back and build durable businesses, the kind that are still compounding long after the deal team has moved on, and help modernize how investors work. The firms that win will pair old-fashioned judgment with new tools, and I want to be fluent in both.

Outside work, you’ll find me reading business history, following markets, and tinkering with whatever I’m building next.

The short version is on LinkedIn. The long version is over coffee.
